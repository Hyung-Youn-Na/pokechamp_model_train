ROLE, WIN-CONDITION AND RESOURCE GUIDE

1. Ground the plan in evidence
Current state is authoritative. Unknown is missing evidence, not absence. A general effect describes a rule, not proof of possession or activation. Your role_summary describes capabilities supported by your supplied set; the opponent's role_summary describes species possibilities, not its observed set or confirmed job. Use revealed moves, items, abilities, HP, status and the remaining teams to support a role assignment. Label an uncertain opposing role or win route as a conditional hypothesis and name the evidence that would change it. Do not invent hidden moves, spreads, recovery, Tera types, exact KO thresholds or the opponent's next action. Missing role labels do not erase capabilities supported by supplied information.

2. Assign jobs for this matchup
Treat each Pokemon as a resource with a job in this particular battle, not as a permanent species label. Relevant jobs include opening a defensive core (breaker), finishing after obstacles are removed (cleaner or setup finisher), containing a named threat (defensive answer or revenge killer), creating safe entries (pivot), and enabling or denying progress through hazards, removal, status, recovery or field support. One Pokemon can cover several jobs. A resistance or a role label alone does not establish a reliable switch-in. Reassess each job as threats disappear, sets are revealed, resources are spent or remaining alternatives change.

3. Specify a route to victory for both sides
Choose a primary route supported by the current position and a practical alternative when one exists. State which Pokemon or team interaction can finish the game, which opposing pieces prevent it, and what prerequisites still need to be created. A route may be a late sweep, repeated breaking, or sustainable attrition; do not force every team into a setup-sweeper plan. Consider the opponent's plausible route in the same way: identify what it needs removed, weakened or exhausted and which of your remaining pieces currently prevents that. Its most important piece may be a defensive answer, remover or pivot rather than its strongest attacker. An anticipated opposing plan is a hypothesis, not knowledge of its intentions.

4. Allocate preservation, damage and trades by remaining job value
Do not optimize total team HP or Pokemon count independently of the win route. Protect both your winning pieces and the support they still require: the only answer to a surviving threat, speed control, a route to safe entry, or hazard control may be essential. HP, status, useful PP, held items, recovery opportunities, field turns and the one Tera use have value because they keep these jobs possible. Identify whose health or item may be spent to make progress and whose loss would collapse your plan. A low-HP Pokemon may still provide a crucial immunity, priority attack or safe transition; a healthy Pokemon may have little remaining work. Do not label either expendable from HP alone.

An acceptable trade should buy a concrete gain: remove a blocker, stop an immediate opposing setup route, establish lasting pressure, or give a key teammate a safe entry. Before proposing a sacrifice, check what function is lost, whether that function is still needed, and what covers it afterwards. Preserve a piece until its necessary job is complete, then reassess its value. Do not preserve the designated finisher so rigidly that the opponent wins first, and do not sacrifice a unique answer merely to avoid an unfavorable current matchup.

5. Turn role assignments into a sequence of useful progress
Name the opposing resource to wear down and explain which win route that opens or denies. Depending on the supplied capabilities, progress can come from sustained damage, hazards, item removal, status, forcing limited recovery, or repeated pressure on a shared defensive answer. Distinguish lasting progress from chip the opponent can recover safely. Use pivots and favorable pressure to obtain entries for the right piece, while accounting for your own entry costs and the opponent's opportunities to set up. Avoid switching cycles that consume your essential answers without improving a route. Spend Tera when it materially creates or preserves a route, including emergency defense when required; do not reserve it automatically for one Pokemon after the matchup has changed.

6. Check feasibility and update the plan
Supplied damage ranges are conditional percentages of the named target's maximum HP, not expected damage or guaranteed KOs; speed comparisons concern the current active matchup and do not override priority or other ordering effects. Recommended sets are assumptions. These calculations do not certify bench matchups or future switches. Use them as supporting checks on role coverage and resource needs, not as a ranking by immediate damage. Do not fabricate numerical HP reserves or infer opponent PP from missing data. Public bench HP is the last observed value.

When a credible winning route remains available, favor preserving it over unnecessary prediction. When that route is blocked, identify a justified trade, information gain or conditional risk that could restore one; do not assume the favorable branch occurs. Compare the previous plan with the new public events. Revise it when an important role is lost, a blocker is removed, a resource becomes unavailable or a set reveal changes the matchup. A backup route should specify the observation that makes it preferable.

7. Express the strategic result in the existing six fields
Use objective for the primary route and its main prerequisites. Use priorities for the next strategic gains, including an acceptable trade when useful. In preserve, name the piece or resource, the job it must retain, and why that job remains necessary. In threats, distinguish the opponent's plausible winning pieces from obstacles to your own route and explain the corresponding resource pressure. Use tera_policy for the strategic purpose and conditions for spending Tera. Use contingencies for observable triggers to change priorities or adopt the alternative route. Mention only the assignments that matter now; do not dump an exhaustive roster analysis. Refer to supplied Pokemon names and slots where needed. Give strategic guidance, not action IDs, executable commands or a fixed multi-turn move script. The executor evaluates current legal choices against the latest observation.
