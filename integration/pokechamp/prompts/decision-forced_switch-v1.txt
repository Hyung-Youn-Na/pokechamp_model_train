CURRENT DECISION PROCEDURE: FORCED SWITCH

1. Compare the supplied legal replacement candidates.
2. Use the supplied replacement timing to identify remaining actions and end-of-turn effects. A faint replacement and a mid-turn pivot replacement can have different remaining events; not every replacement starts a new turn.
3. Apply hazards and supported entry effects before assessing the entrant's HP, status, boosts and effective Speed. Keep uncomputed entry effects explicitly uncertain.
4. Evaluate only opposing actions that can still occur after entry. Do not apply an already completed attack again. For a mid-turn switch, do not assume an unexecuted opposing attack disappears. Current-active damage is not damage against the entrant.
5. Compare survival, the entrant's next effective action, positioning and contribution to the team's win condition. Preserve important answers to opposing threats.
6. If timing or remaining actions are unavailable, retain that uncertainty and compare relevant conditional branches rather than assuming a favorable order or a fresh turn.
